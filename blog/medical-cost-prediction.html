What Makes Medical Insurance Expensive?

Predicting annual medical cost and explaining every quote with machine learning.

Problem

Insurers need to price premiums that cover expected medical costs without overcharging customers. But a price is only defensible if it can be explained - to the customer who pays it and to the regulator who reviews it. So the challenge is twofold: predict a person's annual medical cost accurately, and explain how each prediction was reached.

Solution

Using the public insurance dataset (1,300+ records with age, sex, BMI, children, smoking status and region), I built a linear regression model and explained its predictions with SHAP values. The whole project follows the CRISP-DM process, from business understanding through to a deployable model.

Tech Stack

  • Python
  • scikit-learn
  • statsmodels
  • SHAP
  • pandas

The Questions I Asked

1. Can we predict annual cost well enough to price premiums?
Yes. A linear model with the right features reached an R² of about 0.91 and a typical error (RMSE) near $4,100 - comfortably past the 0.80 target I set at the start.

2. What drives medical cost the most?
Smoking - but not on its own. The combination of smoking and a high BMI dominates everything else, followed by age. The chart below ranks how much each factor pushes a prediction up or down.

3. Does BMI affect everyone equally?
No - and this was the key insight. For non-smokers, BMI barely moves the bill. For smokers, a higher BMI raises cost sharply (see the title chart). Capturing this interaction is what lifted the model from 0.81 to 0.91.

4. Can we explain a single customer's quote?
Yes. SHAP breaks any individual prediction into the exact dollar contribution of each attribute, so an insurer can point to precisely why one person's estimate is higher than another's.

Takeaway

A simple, transparent model beat a more complex Random Forest here (0.91 vs 0.88). When a decision has to be explained, an interpretable model that you can defend is worth more than a black box that you cannot.
